# 1.6 Placeholder Character: Implementation Plan

> Status: **done (2026-09-28).** Parent: [implementation-plan.md](../implementation-plan.md) step 1.6.

## Goal

The grey-box capsule becomes a tiger (standing in for the Cat form of the vertical slice) whose animation follows its movement: it stands and breathes when still, walks, trots and gallops with speed, leans into turns, tilts when speeding up and stopping, swings its tail, and looks at the cursor. It's a placeholder until the 9.3 art pass, but it has to look like the direction of the game (no low-poly), sit in the 1.5 lighting, and stay readable.

When it's done, every room scene (greybox, gym) shows the tiger by default, a pane switch brings the capsule back, the simulation doesn't change (every replay fixture passes untouched), and `pnpm check` passes.

Out of scope:

- Blender animation work (9.3), and a dedicated idle clip (the idle is a rest pose plus procedural breathing)
- changing the player's collision footprint or speed (a capsule footprint or per-form stats are 10.1 / 2.4; the speed stays 7 m/s until the 1.7 feel pass)
- the general asset pipeline: a manifest, loading screen, R2 (9.1). This step adds one model and the smallest loader that serves it.
- animations for abilities (pounce, attacks): 2.3 / 2.5. The attack, howl and eat clips are kept in the file but unused.
- the druid's human form, and other forms (10.x)

## The model

**"Tiger rebuilt"** by kenchoo on Sketchfab (CC-BY 4.0): https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/tiger-rebuilt-6b5d14b2de984ffcb00ee00e404ad208. It mixes a "Tiger" upload (no longer on Sketchfab) with "White Tiger (RIGGED ANIMATED)" by MotionStreamStudios (CC-BY 4.0): https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/white-tiger-rigged-animated-9dd099d283e54f99b7cbd40b531b1a29.

**How we got here:** the plan first picked kenchoo's "black panther" for its look. Inspecting it showed a single 16.5 s clip with a good idle but **no gait cycle at all** (each foot steps once or twice in the whole clip: a few shuffling steps, a crouch, a step back), so there was nothing to loop for walking. We switched to the tiger, which has real locomotion.

What the original `.glb` contains (inspected 2026-09-28; exported by Babylon.js 6.38):

- **Rig:** 3ds Max Biped, 48 joints, one skin, in cm (hip height 93, 2.4 m nose to tail tip, about 1.7 m without the tail). It faces −X in the file.
  - tail: `Bip01 Tail`, `Tail1` … `Tail3`; spine: `Bip01 Spine`, `Spine1`, `Spine2`; neck and head: `Bip01 Neck`, `Bip01 Head`; ears/jaw: `Ponytail1/2`, `Bone01` … `Bone05`
  - legs: `Bip01 L/R Thigh/Calf/HorseLink/Foot/Toe0` (hind), `Clavicle/UpperArm/Forearm/Hand/Finger0` (front)
- **Meshes:** the skinned body (14.9k vertices) and the head (9.4k vertices, unskinned, parented under `Bip01 HeadNub`, so it follows the head).
- **Materials:** `Tiger_Default` (1024² base color + roughness) and `Mouth_mat` (a 2048² base color, 6.2 MB), both with `KHR_materials_specular`; the body's specular is 0.
- **Clips** (all in place, all loop):

| Clip | Length | Ground speed (from planted feet, real size) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walk | 1.63 s | ~0.55 m/s | **walk** |
| Walk Fast | 0.75 s | ~1.8 m/s | **trot** |
| Run | 0.5 s | ~4–5 m/s (flight phases; judged by eye) | **run** (gallop) |
| Attack / Howl / Eat | 4.0 / 2.6 / 6.6 s | – | kept, unused; frame 0 of Howl/Eat is a calm standing pose, which becomes the **idle** |

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Character | **A feline (Cat form)** | Matches the Cat-first vertical slice. The druid's human form waits for the hub. |
| Look | **No low-poly, even for placeholders** | Low-poly isn't the direction of the game. Realistic or painterly only. |
| Source | **Free CC-BY, committed** | Sketchfab, with a CREDITS row naming every author. Works on Previews and production today, no R2. |
| Model | **kenchoo's Tiger rebuilt** | Switched from the black panther, which has no gait cycle (see above). |
| Gaits | **Idle ↔ walk ↔ trot ↔ run, blended by speed** | Each clip's playback rate follows the ground speed so the feet roughly stick; the idle is the rest pose plus procedural breathing. Some foot sliding is accepted until 9.3. |
| Pipeline | **Offline script, output committed** | A Node script (`scripts/build-models.ts`, glTF-Transform) turns the original `.glb` into a game-ready `public/models/tiger.glb`. Only the output is committed; the source is re-downloadable and noted in CREDITS. |
| Size | **Scale the cat to fit the footprint** | The sim stays as is (circle, radius 0.4). A view scale (tunable) makes it fit reasonably; the head and rump may poke past the circle against walls. |
| Readability | **Rim light** | Chosen for the black panther (rim + glowing eyes). The tiger's orange already stands out against the cold moonlight, so only the tunable fresnel rim (WGSL plugin) stays; its eyes have no mask to glow from. |
| Procedural | **Lean into turns, head looks at cursor, accel/decel tilt, tail follow-through** | All view-only and tunable. |
| Loading | **Preload once at startup** | The shell loads the model into a cached `AssetContainer` before the first scene; scene setup instantiates a copy synchronously on every run. |
| Capsule | **Pane toggle "Placeholder body"** | The tiger by default; the View folder switches back to the capsule. A `footprint` debug-draw category shows the collision circle. |

### Defaults I picked (object if you disagree)

- **View-only.** Animation, the procedural layers and materials are rendering. The view reads the player's interpolated mesh (position, facing) and the input's aim point; nothing touches the world, the RNG or the recording, so no replay fixture changes. Speed and turning come from the interpolated mesh's motion per frame (so replays and seeks look the same), and the procedural springs run on frame time: a paused game stays still, a seek snaps them.
- **The pipeline script** `scripts/build-models.ts` (run with `pnpm models:build`):
  - reads the original `.glb` from a gitignored `assets-src/tiger/tiger-rebuilt.glb` (the download link in the script header and CREDITS)
  - renames the clips `walk`, `trot`, `run`, `attack`, `howl`, `eat`, and adds `idle`: the rest pose from frame 0 of Howl, as a two-key clip
  - keeps the rig and the clips as they are (they're in place and loop already); the view handles the cm scale and the −X facing
  - materials: drops `KHR_materials_specular` (a zero specular looked flat under the moon), single-sided where it holds up
  - textures to WebP (sharp): the body's 1024² maps stay 1024², the head's 2048² map becomes 1024²; prunes, dedups, and quantizes vertex data where it's safe
  - writes `public/models/tiger.glb` and prints a summary (clips with durations, vertex count, size). Target: under 3 MB.
  - new devDependencies: `@gltf-transform/core`, `@gltf-transform/functions`, `@gltf-transform/extensions`, `sharp`
- **Loading:** `@babylonjs/loaders` (the glTF loader only, a side-effect import) becomes a dependency. `render/models.ts` loads `/models/tiger.glb` once into an `AssetContainer` during boot (before the first scene), and exposes an instantiate helper (`instantiateModelsToScene`, with its own skeleton and animation groups per copy). If it fails to load, room scenes fall back to the capsule with a console warning.
- **The animation driver** is split like the camera rig:
  - `render/locomotionAnim.ts`, Babylon-free and tested: given the ground speed, how fast the facing turns, and the change in speed, returns the blend weights (idle / walk / trot / run), the shared gait phase rate, and the procedural targets (lean, tilt), with springs for smoothing. Numbers in an `anim` tunable group.
  - `render/tiger.ts` applies them: animation group weights and speed ratios, and bone offsets after the animation (Babylon's `onAfterAnimationsObservable`): the spine (lean, tilt, breathing), neck and head (look at the cursor, clamped), and the tail chain (a damped spring lagging behind the body's turn).
- **Speed mapping:** each gait has a natural ground speed (a tunable, measured from its stride at real size and multiplied by the view scale). Between two gaits the weights cross-fade by speed, and all playing gaits share one normalized phase so their footfalls line up (like a blend tree); the playback rate is speed / that blended natural speed, clamped. Below a small threshold it's the idle. Stride matching is judged by eye against the floor grid.
- **Facing:** the model's −X forward axis is turned to the sim's facing (+Z) once in the view; the body follows the interpolated transform, so turning speed stays the sim's `turnRate`.
- **Head look:** toward the aim point on the ground, clamped (about ±60° yaw), eased; it relaxes to straight ahead when the cursor isn't the player's to use.
- **Materials in the rig:** the tiger's materials are PBR, so they get the height fog like everything else; it casts moon shadows, receives shadows, and stays excluded from the player light like the capsule (`setLightTarget` `exclude`). The rim plugin adds `rim.color`, `rim.strength`, `rim.power` tunables.
- **Scale:** a `tiger.scale` tunable, default about 0.5 (a 1.2 m tiger nose to tail tip, the body roughly spanning the 0.8 m circle); the capsule's dimensions stay as they are.
- **Pane (View folder):** "placeholder body" (the capsule instead of the tiger), persisted like the other view settings and switched live; the `footprint` debug-draw category (the collision circle and facing), hidden by default like every category. The entity picker keeps working: the tiger hangs under an empty root mesh bound to the player through `bindMesh`, and `entityOf` walks up a mesh's parents.
- **Stress** is unchanged (boxes).
- **Tests** (pure logic only): the gait mapping (weights sum to 1, idle at rest, each gait at its natural speed, rate clamped), the springs (settle, and the result barely depends on the frame rate across 30/60/144 Hz), the head-look clamp. No Babylon in tests.

## Architecture

```
scripts/
  build-models.ts      original .glb → public/models/tiger.glb (clip names, idle pose, materials, WebP)
assets-src/            gitignored: downloaded source models
public/models/
  tiger.glb            the game-ready model (committed, CC-BY)
render/
  models.ts            boot-time glTF preload into an AssetContainer; instantiate per scene
  locomotionAnim.ts    pure: speed → gait weights/rate, procedural targets, springs       (tested)
  tiger.ts             the tiger view: instance, gait blending, bone layers, rim
  rimMaterial.ts       WGSL plugin: fresnel rim on PBR
  meshSync.ts          entityOf walks up parents
scenes/
  arena.view.ts        the tiger (or the capsule) as the player body; footprint debug draw
debug/
  pane.ts, devtools.ts, persist.ts   View folder: placeholder body
shell.ts               awaits the model preload before the first scene
CREDITS.md             the tiger row
```

Rule 1 holds: `locomotionAnim.ts` imports nothing from Babylon, and the sim is untouched.

## Steps

1. Branch `1.6-placeholder-character`. The original `.glb` in `assets-src/tiger/`, `assets-src/` gitignored, the CREDITS row.
2. `scripts/build-models.ts` + `pnpm models:build` → `public/models/tiger.glb`.
3. `render/models.ts`: preload in the shell, instantiate in the room view under a root bound to the player; scale and facing aligned; shadows, light exclusion.
4. `render/locomotionAnim.ts` with tests; `render/tiger.ts` blends idle ↔ walk ↔ trot ↔ run from speed.
5. Procedural layers: breathing, lean into turns, accel/decel tilt, tail follow-through, head look at the cursor.
6. Readability: the rim plugin; check in the value view and next to the stand-in enemy.
7. Pane: "placeholder body" and the `footprint` debug-draw category.
8. Browser check (acceptance below) in the greybox and the gym, both presets, a replay playback and seek, pause and frame step, restarts and scene switches. `pnpm check` and `pnpm build`.
9. Tune the `anim`, `rim` and `tiger` numbers by eye; the user plays and tunes further, and "Copy changes" goes back into the code.
10. Docs:
    - AGENTS.md: layout (`render/models.ts`, `locomotionAnim.ts`, `tiger.ts`, `rimMaterial.ts`, `scripts/build-models.ts`, `assets-src/`, `public/models/`), the `pnpm models:build` command, the placeholder body toggle in Dev tools
    - implementation notes here, tick 1.6, devlog entry `devlog/1.6-placeholder-character/` (screenshots: the tiger idle and galloping in the greybox, a turn with lean and tail, the head looking at the cursor, the value view, the capsule/footprint comparison)

## Implementation notes

- **Verified in headless Chrome** (agent-browser, real WebGPU) on the dev server and the production preview (`?debug`):
  - The tiger stands in the greybox and the gym, faces the sim's facing, and walks, trots and gallops with speed (checked at forced top speeds of 0.28, 0.92 and 7 m/s). The head turns toward the cursor on either side.
  - With grain and the light's breathing turned off, two frames 0.7 s apart while paused are pixel-identical, and a frame step changes the image. Only the frame-time breathing of the player light (1.5) moves while paused.
  - Mesh, material, texture, skeleton, animation group, transform node and geometry counts are identical after five restarts and a round trip through the gym. The model is loaded once.
  - Hovering and clicking the tiger with the picker selects the player, and the outline covers the model's meshes (`entityOf` walks up parents; the highlight outlines the bound mesh's visible children).
  - "Placeholder body" swaps the tiger and the capsule live; the `footprint` category draws the collision circle and facing under either.
- **Every replay fixture passed without `pnpm replay:update`**: the sim didn't change. `pnpm check` passes (283 tests, 15 new in `locomotionAnim.test.ts`).
- **Bundle:** `@babylonjs/loaders` goes through `registerBuiltInLoaders()`, so the glTF loader and its extensions load lazily. Rolldown split the shared Babylon code into many more chunks as a result: the first load went from 32 to 228 files, 1.48 → 1.58 MB gzipped, plus the 1.8 MB model. Worth a look in 9.6 (deep imports, fewer glTF extensions).
- **Deviations from the plan:**
  - Model: the plan picked kenchoo's black panther; its single clip has no gait cycle, so we switched to kenchoo's "Tiger rebuilt" (see "The model"). The glowing eyes went with the panther: the orange tiger reads without them, and its eyes have no mask to glow from.
  - The clips are sampled by hand (`Animation.evaluate`) on a new `ctx.viewTime()` instead of played by Babylon's animation groups, which run on wall-clock time and wouldn't stop while paused. Gait inputs (speed, turn rate, acceleration) come from the player's components each tick, not from the interpolated mesh.
  - The player's warm light lights the tiger (it's low enough), with a `tiger.light` tunable (0.5) scaling the direct light on its fur so its back doesn't blow out; the capsule stays excluded.
  - New tunables: `tiger.offset` (the model's origin is at its hips; 0.35 m real size sits the torso on the circle) and `tiger.light`.
  - The capsule hangs under the tiger's root mesh (bound to the player), so both follow the entity and either can show.
  - `sharp` is pinned to the version glTF-Transform pulls in, so only one libvips is loaded (two made macOS warn about duplicate classes).
- **Starting numbers, for the 1.7 feel pass:** `tiger.scale` 0.5, natural speeds walk 0.55 / trot 1.85 / run 5 m/s at real size (measured from planted feet; the gallop's by eye), `anim.maxRate` 1.8 (at 7 m/s the gallop plays at 1.8× and the feet slide a little), lean 0.6 of the balanced bank up to 14°, tilt 0.12°/(m/s²) up to 7°, tail 18°/(rad/s) with a 1.6 Hz, 0.35-damped spring, head ±60°.
- **Found, not fixed:** the tiger's orange is close to the red-orange of the stand-in danger telegraphs (3.2 and the real Cat model should keep them apart).
