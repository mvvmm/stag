# 1.4 Camera Follow: Implementation Plan

> Status: **done (2026-09-27).** Parent: [implementation-plan.md](../implementation-plan.md) step 1.4.

## Goal

The camera stops being a hard follow and starts helping you see what you're aiming at:

- **Cursor look-ahead:** the view shifts toward the cursor, up to a few meters, driven by the cursor's position on screen (not the aim point on the ground).
- **Tight follow, smooth offset:** the player stays pinned to their spot. Only the look-ahead offset eases in and out, so dodges and stops stay crisp.
- **Bounds:** the camera's look-at point is clamped to the room, inset by a margin, so the look-ahead can't swing the view far into the void past the walls.

When it's done, both rooms follow the player with look-ahead in both presets, the view never jitters or lags behind the player, and it stops pushing past the room edges. The simulation doesn't change: every replay fixture passes untouched. `pnpm check` must pass.

Out of scope:

- screen shake (2.7). The rig is built so an extra offset can be added later.
- zoom, camera rotation, or any player-controlled camera
- look-ahead toward the movement direction
- per-preset tuning (judged at the 1.7 feel gate) and a player setting to turn look-ahead off (16.1 / 16.3)
- the "keep the whole view inside the room" style of bounds, and soft bounds
- lighting and fog, which will hide the void past the walls anyway (1.5)

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Look-ahead | **Toward the cursor** | Threats come from where you aim, so the view leans that way. V Rising / Diablo-with-offset feel. |
| Offset source | **Cursor's screen position** | Offset = the cursor's position relative to the screen center (normalized, with a dead zone near the center) times a max distance in meters, along the camera's ground-projected right/forward axes. Using the aim point on the ground would make the camera and the aim chase each other (the camera moves toward the cursor, which moves the ground point under it further out). The screen position has no feedback and feels the same at any pitch or FOV. |
| Follow | **Tight follow, smooth offset** | The player part of the target follows the interpolated mesh with no lag by default. Only the look-ahead offset is smoothed. A `camera.follow` lag tunable exists (default 0) to try a softer follow at 1.7. |
| Bounds | **Clamp the look-at point to the room, inset** | The final target (player + offset) is clamped to the room rect shrunk by `camera.boundsInset` on every side. Near a wall the player moves off-center, and the look-ahead can't push the view toward the wall. The scene gives the rect; scenes without one aren't clamped. |
| Presets | **Same behavior in both** | One set of tunables for `mmo` and `moba`. If the view shifting toward every click target feels off in `moba`, we split it at 1.7. |

### Defaults I picked (object if you disagree)

- **View-only.** The camera is not simulation state: it runs on frame time in the view, never touches the world, the RNG or the recording. Aim is already quantized into the recorded input, and WASD reads the camera's yaw, which the rig doesn't change. So no replay fixture changes, and the check for "the sim didn't move" is simply that they all still pass.
- **Rig math is pure and tested** (`render/cameraRig.ts`, no Babylon import, so Vitest can run it in Node):
  - `lookAheadOffset(cursor, viewport, yaw, { distance, deadZone })`: the cursor relative to the screen center, divided by half the *shorter* viewport side (so the reach is the same distance left/right and up/down on screen, and a wide window reaches a bit further sideways), clamped to length 1. The dead zone is remapped (`(len − deadZone) / (1 − deadZone)`) so the offset starts at 0 at its edge with no step. Screen up maps to the camera's ground forward. Returns a world `Vec2`.
  - `ease(current, target, lag, dt)`: exponential smoothing, `1 − exp(−dt / lag)`, frame-rate independent; `lag = 0` snaps. It's view code, so `Math.exp` is fine (the dmath rule is for the sim).
  - `clampToRect(point, rect, inset)`: clamps to the rect shrunk by `inset`; if the shrunk rect is inverted on an axis (a room smaller than twice the inset), it pins that axis to the rect's center.
  - `CameraRig` state: `{ follow: Vec2, offset: Vec2 }` plus `update(input, dt)` → look-at point, and `snap()`.
- **Order per frame** (in the shell, where `updateCamera` runs now, after mesh sync): follow point eases toward the scene's target (instant by default) → target offset from the cursor → offset eases toward it → sum → clamp to bounds → `updateCamera(camera, lookAt)` places the camera as before (pitch, yaw, distance, FOV). Clamping after smoothing keeps the clamp hard, so the view can't overshoot a wall while the offset eases.
- **Tunables** (added to the existing `camera` group):
  - `lookAhead`: max offset in meters, default 3 (0–8, step 0.1). 0 turns look-ahead off.
  - `deadZone`: fraction of the half-screen around the center with no offset, default 0.15 (0–0.9).
  - `lookAheadLag`: offset smoothing time constant in seconds, default 0.25 (0–1).
  - `follow`: player follow lag in seconds, default 0 (0–0.5).
  - `bounds`: checkbox, default on.
  - `boundsInset`: meters, default 4 (−10–15, step 0.1). Negative lets the look-at point go past the walls. With the greybox (30×20) that lets the camera travel ±11 m east–west and ±6 m north–south. We tune it in play.
- **No look-ahead when the cursor isn't usable:** the target offset goes to 0 (and eases back) when the pointer is outside the canvas or the page loses focus, while a replay is playing back (the live mouse isn't the one that was recorded), and while the debug free camera renders. While the game is paused, the target offset holds, so pausing freezes the view instead of letting it wander with the mouse.
- **Snapping:** the rig snaps (no easing, offset reset to 0) on every scene load and restart, and whenever the follow target jumps more than 2 m in one frame (a teleport, a replay seek or take over), so the camera never swoops across the room.
- **Scene API:** a new `ctx.setCameraBounds(rect: { minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ } | null)` next to `setCameraTarget`. The shared room view sets it from the `room` entity (`±width/2`, `±depth/2`). Scenes that don't set bounds (stress) aren't clamped.
- **Occlusion fading** keeps using the player as its focus, not the look-at point, so it still fades whatever hides the player.
- **Debug draw:** a new `camera` category (hidden by default, drawn from the shell's view code): the look-at point, an arrow from the player to it (the offset), the target offset as a faint point, and the bounds rect after the inset. Pane readout isn't needed; the entity pane doesn't show the camera, since it isn't an entity.
- **Aim stays correct:** aim is still projected through last frame's camera at the start of the frame, which is what was under the cursor. The camera now moves more between frames, so we check in play that the aim point sits exactly under the cursor while the view shifts.

## Architecture

```
render/
  cameraRig.ts       pure (no Babylon): lookAheadOffset, ease, clampToRect, createCameraRig   (tested)
  scene.ts           + camera tunables (lookAhead, deadZone, lookAheadLag, follow, bounds, boundsInset)
scenes/
  scene.ts           + ctx.setCameraBounds(rect | null)
  arena.view.ts      sets bounds from the room entity
shell.ts             rig per load (snap on load), feeds cursor/viewport/yaw, look-ahead gating
                     (pointer, focus, pause, replay playback, free camera), camera debug draw
```

Rule 1 holds: nothing in the sim changes, and `cameraRig.ts` imports nothing from Babylon so its tests run in Node.

## Steps

1. `render/cameraRig.ts` with tests: dead zone and its edge (no step), clamp to unit length, screen axes to world axes at yaw 0 and 90°, `ease` with lag 0 and frame-rate independence (one 1/30 s step ≈ two 1/60 s steps), clamp with a normal and an inverted rect, snapping on a big jump.
2. The new `camera` tunables, `ctx.setCameraBounds`, and the room view setting it.
3. Shell: build the rig, feed it each frame, gate the look-ahead, snap on load/restart/jumps. `updateCamera` takes the rig's look-at point.
4. The `camera` debug-draw category.
5. Browser check (acceptance below) in both rooms and both presets, including pause, free camera, replay playback and seek. `pnpm check` and `pnpm build`.
6. Play it by hand and tune the defaults (distance, dead zone, lag, inset). Paste the pane's "Copy changes" back into the code.
7. Docs:
   - AGENTS.md: layout (`render/cameraRig.ts`), the `setCameraBounds` part of rule 10
   - implementation notes here, tick 1.4, devlog entry `devlog/1.4-camera-follow/` (screenshots: centered view, full look-ahead toward a corner with the `camera` category on, clamped against a wall, the gym)

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Moving the cursor away from the center shifts the view toward it, up to `lookAhead` meters, smoothly, with no step at the dead zone's edge. Cursor at the center: the player is centered.
- [x] Running, stopping and turning keep the player pinned (with `follow` at 0): no lag, overshoot or jitter at 60 Hz and at uncapped frame rates.
- [x] The aim point stays under the cursor while the view shifts (the ground-aim debug marker and the click marker land where you click).
- [x] Near a wall the view stops at `boundsInset` and the player moves off-center; the look-ahead can't push past it. Toggling `bounds` off lets the view go past.
- [x] Moving the cursor out of the window or blurring the page eases the view back to the player. Pausing freezes the view. The free camera and replay playback don't get the live cursor's look-ahead.
- [x] Loading, restarting, switching scenes, and seeking a replay snap the camera to the player with no swoop.
- [x] Every tunable updates live from the pane. The `camera` category draws the look-at point, offset and inset bounds, starts hidden, and costs nothing in player builds.
- [x] Every replay fixture passes unchanged in `pnpm test` (the sim didn't change).
- [x] `pnpm check` passes, and `cameraRig.ts` imports no Babylon.
- [x] No new third-party assets (so no CREDITS.md rows).
- [x] Docs are updated, the devlog entry is written, 1.4 is ticked, and the PR is open with the checks and Preview green.

## Implementation notes

- **Verified in headless Chrome** (agent-browser, real WebGPU) on the dev server, reading the camera's look-at point every frame:
  - With the cursor at the right edge of the shorter screen side, the view settled at exactly player + 3 m, and reached 1.896 m after 250 ms (63%, one time constant). A corner cursor gave a 3 m offset diagonally. 60 px from the center (inside the 0.15 dead zone) gave nothing.
  - Running east with WASD, the look-at point stayed on the interpolated player mesh with an error of 0 in every frame.
  - Next to the east side of the greybox the look-at point stopped at x = 11 (15 − 4). With `bounds` off it went on to the player + 3 m. Near the south wall it stopped at z = −6.
  - Moving the pointer outside the canvas eased the view back onto the player. Pausing held the view while the cursor moved, and unpausing let it go on. Replay playback kept the view on the player whatever the live cursor did, and look-ahead came back after exit. A restart snapped the view onto the player, and the look-ahead eased in from zero.
  - The stress scene has no bounds and wasn't clamped.
- **Every replay fixture passed without `pnpm replay:update`**, which is the proof that the camera is view-only.
- **One bug, caught in the browser, not the tests:** the shell first passed the whole `CAMERA` tunables object as the look-ahead options. Its `distance` is the camera's 17.8 m distance from the target, not `lookAhead`, so the view swung to the bounds. The options are now built explicitly.
- **The defaults haven't been tuned by hand yet** (step 6). Things to try at the 1.7 feel gate:
  - The inset is the same on every side, but the camera looks north, so near the south wall the near half of the screen still shows ~8 m of floor past it, while the north side shows little. A separate inset for the near side, or fog (1.5), would fix that.
  - In the smaller gym (24×16), an inset of 4 m leaves the look-at point only ±8 × ±4 m to move in, so the camera barely moves there.
