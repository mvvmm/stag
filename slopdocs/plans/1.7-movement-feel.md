# 1.7 Movement Feel Pass: Implementation Plan

> Status: **built (2026-10-02), waiting for the user's playtest** (the gate). Parent: [implementation-plan.md](../implementation-plan.md) step 1.7.

## Goal

Running around the empty arena is fun on its own (the gate), and the control scheme is decided.

The interview came to this: **the mechanics are good** (speed, acceleration, near-instant League-style turning), so the remaining stiffness comes from the tiger's look. It turns like a rigid 2 m pole at 1800°/s. It has no start or stop motion. At the 4 m/s cruise it shows a mushy blend of trot and gallop. And tiny moves flicker through the gaits. This step makes the motion fluid, procedurally on the 1.6 rig, the way League's cats (Nidalee) stay fluid at instant turn rates. It also fixes one real sim bug (A/D spam spins the tiger in full circles) and gives the scheme choice a pause menu.

When it's done:

- both schemes are kept as a player setting, WASD by default, switched from a minimal pause menu (Esc) or with M
- spamming A/D (or reversing a right-click) flips back and forth through the side you came from, never in full circles
- the tiger bends through turns, finishes its step and settles when stopping, pushes off when starting, cruises in a slowed gallop, and shuffles for tiny moves
- the user plays the Preview and signs the gate off by merging

Out of scope:

- new mechanics or retuning `player.*` (the user tunes further while playing; any change is recorded as a tunable diff)
- the real Cat model and hand-made animation (9.3). For it, the note is that **the body should be shorter than the tiger's**, like Nidalee's cougar: the scale is right, the length isn't.
- final keybinds and dodge (2.2), the full pause and settings menus (6.1, 16.1)
- the camera (no complaints in the interview)

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Control scheme | **Both, as a setting** | Every ability and enemy has to work with both from now on. |
| Default | **WASD (mmo)** | The moba scheme is opt-in. |
| Setting UI | **A minimal pause menu now** | Esc opens it: Resume, and Controls (WASD / Right-click). M stays as a shortcut. It grows into the 6.1 pause menu. |
| Mechanics | **Unchanged** | The user finds them good. The only sim change is the reversal tie-break below. |
| Approach | **Procedural, on the 1.6 rig** | View-only layers on the existing clips. What we learn carries over to the 9.3 rig. |
| Turns | **Sim yaw stays exact, the body bends** | Like Nidalee: near-instant turns, but the body has slack. The root keeps the sim's facing, the shoulders lead, the hips trail through a spine bend, and small body rotations soak up the snap. |
| Reversals (sim) | **Swing through the side you came from** | A ~180° turn goes through the direction you were last moving across it. Moving W and then spamming A/D flips back and forth through W. It works the same for right-click reversals. |
| Size | **Unchanged (`tiger.scale` 1)** | A shorter body waits for our own model (9.3). |
| Start / stop | **All four** | Stops finish the step, stop settle, start push-off, and the start goes straight into the gait for the target speed. |
| Micro-moves | **A shuffle step** | Short moves play a step or two at the walk's pace, not a flicker of trot. |
| Cruise gait | **The gallop alone, slowed** | 4 m/s is the run clip at ~0.8×. The trot only shows in passing. |
| Gate | **The user's playtest on the Preview** | I build and check in headless Chrome (slow motion: turns, A/D spam, stops, taps, both schemes). Follow-ups go into this plan, as in 1.6, and merging signs it off. |

### Defaults I picked (object if you disagree)

- **Reversal tie-break (sim, `systems/locomotion.ts`):**
  - The mover gets `turnSide: 1 | -1`: the sign of its last turn step that wasn't a tie-break.
  - When the turn to the target is within `REVERSE_BAND` (10°) of 180°, the facing turns **against** `turnSide`. That undoes the last turn, so the swing goes back through the direction it came from:
    - W → A turns counter-clockwise, so A → D turns clockwise through W, and D → A counter-clockwise through W again.
    - On a right-click path, a reversal swings back through the side of its last corner.
  - Every other turn takes the shortest arc as today and updates `turnSide`. It starts at 1 at spawn.
  - Plain sim data, so snapshots stay exact. The band is a constant, not a tunable (it isn't a feel number).
- **Spine bend (view):**
  - The yaw is spread over the spine bones (pelvis → spine → spine1 → spine2 → neck) as a spring chain, like the tail but running backward from the shoulders.
  - The front follows the sim facing tightly. Each bone toward the hips lags the one ahead, so the hips trail and catch up.
  - The bend is clamped (`anim.bendMax`, ~40° summed). A full reversal shows as a coil whose hips swing round just behind the shoulders, not a pole spinning on its center.
  - A small overshoot in the shoulders (damping below 1) gives the "slight body rotations".
  - New `anim` tunables: `bend` (on/off), `bendFrequency`, `bendDamping`, `bendMax`.
- **Starts:**
  - On a start from (near) standing, a one-shot push-off envelope (~0.2 s, `anim.pushOff`, `pushOffTime`): the hips dip, the nose drops and the body stretches forward. The existing tilt spring carries the rest.
  - The gait weights follow the **target** speed (`mover.desired`, read per tick) rather than the lagged actual speed, so a start goes straight to the gallop instead of walk → trot → run in 0.07 s.
  - The stride phase jumps to the run clip's push-off foot at the start.
- **Stops:**
  - When the speed drops to zero, the gait keeps playing until the next point in its cycle where the feet are planted (measured per clip during the work and stored next to the footfall offsets, capped at `anim.stopMax` ~0.25 s). Only then does it cross-fade into the idle. The feet slide a little, since the root has already stopped.
  - Stop settle: braking pitches the body forward, it rocks back once and settles (an underdamped spring, `anim.settle`, `settleDamping`). The head and tail follow through.
- **Shuffle:**
  - A move commits to the gallop only once it's long enough:
    - right-click: the order's path is longer than `anim.shuffleDistance` (~1 m)
    - WASD: the key has been held longer than `anim.shuffleTime` (~0.12 s); the push-off covers that window
  - Shorter moves play walk steps at the walk's own rate (one or two steps, finishing the step on stop).
- **Cruise:** above the trot, the blend hands over to the run clip by `runFrom` (set so 4 m/s is pure gallop). The run's playback rate follows the speed (~0.8× at 4 m/s), clamped by `minRate`. The trot only shows in passing.
- **Pause menu (`ui/PauseMenu.tsx`):**
  - Esc (the existing `pause` shell action) opens it in both schemes and closes it again. It has a title, Resume, and a Controls choice (WASD / Right-click, the current one marked), with "M switches anytime" under it. The overlay opts into pointer events.
  - A `menuOpen` signal, separate from `loop.paused`:
    - the debug pane's pause and the auto-pause on blur don't open the menu
    - while it's open the sim is paused and game input is ignored
  - In the moba scheme the first Esc only releases the pointer lock (the browser keeps that press), so a second Esc opens the menu. Picking a scheme or Resume closes the menu. A click on the canvas locks the pointer again, as today.
  - The scheme stays saved where M saves it today, and the default for a first visit becomes `mmo` (check what 1.6 stores).
  - Styled with tokens, dark and quiet. It's a placeholder for 6.1.
- **Pure logic, tested, in `render/locomotionAnim.ts`:** the bend chain (lags, then settles, clamped, frame-rate independent), the stop phase (the next planted point after a phase, never backward, capped), the push-off envelope (starts, peaks, ends at 0), the shuffle decision, and the gait weights at cruise (4 m/s → run only).
- **Sim tests:** `locomotion.test.ts` covers the tie-break:
  - A/D spam after W keeps the facing in the W half
  - a reversal after a right turn goes back through the same side
  - non-reversal turns keep the shortest arc
  - `dt` isn't assumed

  `movementReplays.test.ts` adds a headless A/D-spam check (input built in the test, no recorded fixture needed): no facing crosses the far half over 20 flips.
- **Replay fixtures:** reversals now turn the other way in some fixtures, so `pnpm replay:update` rewrites their checkpoints (input unchanged). The behaviour checks in `movementReplays.test.ts` must still pass.
- **cf bump:** per AGENTS.md, bump `cf` to the latest beta at the start of the step. Any friction goes in a log here.

## Implementation notes

- **`cf` was already the latest beta** (1.0.0-beta.10), so there was nothing to bump and no friction to log.
- **The default scheme really was moba.** `DEFAULT_PRESET` in `input/bindings.ts` said `moba`, which is why a fresh browser started with right-click. It's `mmo` now. Saved choices are kept.
- **Reversals (sim):** `mover.turnSide` is the sign of the last turn step outside the reversal band (10° either side of 180°). Inside the band the turn goes against it, the long way if needed. After the first step of a reversal the turn is outside the band and takes over `turnSide`, so the next flip undoes it: moving W, A/D spam sweeps back and forth through W indefinitely. Every replay fixture was rewritten by `pnpm replay:update` (input unchanged; the mover has a new field, and reversals turn the other way). The behaviour checks in `movementReplays.test.ts` still pass, plus a new headless A/D-spam check through the arena sim. Both new locomotion tests fail without the fix.
- **The spine bend** is two springs relative to the sim facing (`stepBend`): the shoulders (7 Hz, damping 0.65, a little overshoot) and the hips following the shoulders (3.5 Hz, at most 40° behind). The whole rig yaws to where the hips point, and Spine1, Spine2 and the neck turn the front back by the bend (40/35/25%). The hind legs and tail hang off the lower spine and the front legs off the neck, so the legs go with their half. The head look is measured from the shoulders. A full reversal can leave the shoulders at most 135° behind.
- **Gait driver** (`stepGaits`, pure and tested), a small state machine (idle, move, shuffle, stop). The shown weights follow the wanted ones (`anim.fade`, 80 ms).
  - **Starting:** the speed the gaits follow is where the body is heading (`mover.desired`) while it speeds up, and it rises at once and eases down, so a start goes straight to the gallop. A start from standing puts the stride on `anim.startPhase` (0, the run's left-hind-forward point; tune by eye) and triggers the push-off: the nose down 6° and the hips dipping 6 cm over 0.22 s.
  - **Stopping:** the gaits keep striding as they were to the lowest "distance from standing" within `anim.stopMax` (0.25 s). Then the idle fades in.
  - **The planted points aren't measured from feet.** Each gait clip is sampled 48 times per cycle at load, scoring how far its pose is from the idle's (the summed angle between bone rotations). The lowest points are where the legs are closest to standing, so stopping there blends into the idle with the least leg motion. This is simpler than tracking feet through the skeleton, works for any rig, and is computed when the model loads.
  - **Stop settle:** braking (decay 80 ms) drives an underdamped spring (2.2 Hz, damping 0.35): the body pitches forward and rocks back once. Accelerating still tilts nose-down, but braking no longer tilts it nose-up.
- **Shuffle** (`stepShuffle`, per tick): a move is a shuffle until a right-click path is longer than 1 m, or a key has been held 0.12 s. A shuffle plays the walk at twice its own rate, and a commit cross-fades into the gait.
- **Cruise:** `tiger.runFrom` (3 m/s): the gallop has fully taken over from the trot by then, so 4 m/s is the run clip alone at 0.8×.
- **Pause menu:**
  - `ui/PauseMenu.tsx`: Resume, then WASD / Right-click (picking one switches and resumes), plus a hint line.
  - `pauseMenu` and `menuActions` live in `ui/signals.ts`. The shell owns both; it opens and closes the menu on the `pause` shell action, and keeps the shown scheme in sync with the live preset (the pane can switch it too).
  - Opening the menu pauses and releases held input and the pointer lock. Closing it resumes.
- **Verified in headless Chrome** (dev server, frame-stepped): a 90° turn shows the shoulders leading and the hips trailing; a stop strides into a standing pose and dips the head; after moving W, eight A/D flips kept the facing within ±90° of W (−0.85 … 1.05 rad) with the body curving through W; the menu opens on Esc, switches schemes, closes on Esc and Resume, `M` works while it's open, and the pane's pause doesn't open it; a replay plays and seeks twice with no divergence or errors. `pnpm check` passes (315 tests, 13 new).
- **Not seen in the browser, only in tests:** the shuffle (right-click needs real pointer events) and the right-click side of the reversal rule. The playtest covers them.
- **Tuning left for the playtest:** in frame steps the shoulders trail the sim facing by ~60° two ticks into a 90° turn (the 7 Hz spring). If turns feel late, raise `anim.bendFrequency`. Also `anim.startPhase` (which foot pushes off) and the push-off and settle sizes.

## Follow-up after the first playtest (2026-10-02)

The user found the pass a clear improvement and asked for three more things about the gallop:

1. The legs move more than the body travels.
2. Nidalee's run is a string of pounces (front legs together, hind legs together) and a little playful.
3. The tiger's rump stays perfectly still while everything around it moves, which looks uncanny.

They're fine with the full-speed run playing slowly at our speed. We measured the run clip with forward kinematics on its bones (in Node, from the glTF), and each complaint had a concrete cause:

- **The run's natural speed was wrong.** While a foot is planted, it slides back at 6.3–7.3 m/s; 5 m/s had been judged by eye. `tiger.runSpeed` 5 → 7, so at 4 m/s the gallop plays at 0.57× and the feet stick.
- **It's a rotary gallop.** The feet land one by one: left hind, right hind a quarter-stride later, right front, left front. The new `anim.bound` (0.8) pulls each left/right pair together in time:
  - Every animation channel knows its leg (the bones under each thigh and clavicle).
  - When the run is sampled, each leg chain is shifted toward its partner (`pairShift`) by the gap between their contact phases. The contact phases are measured from the tiger's own pose at load (`contactPhase`: the circular middle of the samples where the foot is near its lowest).
  - At 1 the hind pair and the front pair each land as one, a bound. The shift only applies to the run; the walk and trot are as the clip has them.
- **The run's hips are pinned.** In the run clip the pelvis isn't animated and the lower spine moves 3 mm. The body motion now comes from the feet (`strideMotion`): the tiger samples its own run pose 48 times per stride at load (and when `bound` changes), reads where each foot is relative to the rig, and works out three curves over the stride, each smoothed around the loop and scaled into [-1, 1]:
  - **lift:** up in flight, down while feet carry weight (`anim.bounce`, 8 cm)
  - **rock:** nose up while only the hind feet are down (the push), nose down on the front landing (`anim.rock`, 5°)
  - **gather:** the back curls when the hind feet come forward under the body (`anim.flex`, 6° at the mid-back, half of it given back at the neck so the head stays level)

  All three are scaled by how much of the gallop is showing, so the walk, trot and idle are unaffected. The whole body moves with them, rump included.
- The stop points are now worked out from the shifted run too, so a stop still lands on a near-standing pose.
- Tests: contact phases, the pair shift (including across the loop's wrap), and the motion curves on synthetic feet (low on landings, nose up on the hind feet, gathered with the hind feet forward). `pnpm check` passes (321 tests), and the replay fixtures are untouched (view-only).
- Checked in headless Chrome: a steady gallop alternates a full stretch (front legs reaching, hind legs back, tail up) with a gather, landing in pairs. Whether it reads as "playful" is for the playtest: `anim.bound`, `bounce`, `rock` and `flex` are the knobs.

## Second follow-up: the back arches (2026-10-02)

The user loved the bound and asked for the torso to bunch up into a slight upside-down V and spread out with each pounce, instead of staying long and stiff.

- **The arch** replaces the first flex (6° at the mid-back, half given back at the neck), which turned out to bend the wrong way: measured in the browser, it sagged the back on the gather.
  - Now three joints share it: Spine1 lifts by the arch, Spine2 turns down by twice that, and the neck lifts by it again. The mid-back peaks at Spine2, and the shoulders, head and front legs keep their angle. The hind legs hang off the lower spine, so they stay as the clip has them.
  - It follows the stride's `gather` curve. Gathered, it arches by `anim.flex` (15°). Spread out, it goes a little past straight (`anim.stretch`, 0.35 of the arch).
- **Checked by measurement, not by eye** (the camera looks down too steeply to judge a back's curve): Spine2's height above the line from Spine1 to the neck, flex on minus flex off, at 16 points of a stride. The first sign gave −13 cm on the gather and +4.8 cm on the stretch at 35°, so it sagged; flipped, at 12° it's +5.7 cm and −1.6 cm. At the 15° default the hump is about 7 cm.

## Experiment: exaggerated pounce (2026-10-02)

The user asked to see it pushed further, "super pouncy and crunched up and playful". As an experiment:

- `anim.bound` 0.8 → 1 (the pairs land as one)
- `bounce` 0.08 → 0.18 m
- `rock` 5 → 12° (its range now goes to 30)
- `flex` 15 → 32°
- `stretch` 0.35 → 0.6

The bounce now only lifts the body from the landings' height (the lift curve rescaled so its lowest point is 0). Before, it also pushed the body down on the landings, which at this size would sink the feet into the floor. To go back, the previous values are above.

**Kept as a preset (2026-10-02).** The user wanted both styles in the pane, with the natural one as the default. The defaults are back to bound 0.8, bounce 0.08, rock 5, flex 15, stretch 0.35. `debug/gallopPresets.ts` adds a "Gallop" command group, built like the movement presets:
- "Natural (defaults)" resets those five `anim` tunables
- "Pouncy" sets the exaggerated values

Like any tunable change, they're recorded in replays. The bounce keeps the lift-only fix, so at 8 cm the natural gallop now rises 0–8 cm from its landings instead of ±8 cm around its old height.

## Third follow-up: stopping with the legs (2026-10-02)

The user found the stop fake: the tiger "rotates back into neutral". That was literal. On a stop the gait strode on for up to 0.25 s, then every bone cross-faded from the gallop pose into the standing pose, so the feet slid and floated into place with no steps.

A stop is now done leg by leg:

- **Per-leg sampling.** A pose is now a list of (clip, phase, weight) entries. The body uses one list; during a stop each leg has its own, so its bones can be in a different clip and stride phase from the rest of the body.
- **Measured at load:** for each gait clip and each leg, how much the foot is on the ground over the stride (`footContact`) and where it touches down (`touchdownPhase`: the start of its longest contact). For each leg, where its foot is highest in the walk (`liftPhase`): the lifted pose of a step.
- **The plan** (`planStop`, pure and tested):
  - A foot on the ground stays planted.
  - A leg in the air within `anim.stopSwingMax` (0.3) of a stride from touching down swings on and lands. The swing runs at the gait's own pace × `anim.stopRate` (1.3), not the slowed cruise; the first try used the cruise and the front legs galloped in place for half a second.
  - A leg further away (it has only just lifted off) steps straight in, now.
  - Once every other foot is down and `anim.stopPause` (0.06 s) has passed, they step in one by one in landing order, `anim.stopStagger` (0.08 s) apart.
- **A step** (`legStopAt`) lasts `anim.stopStep` (0.18 s). The leg blends from its planted pose to the idle's, and through the walk's mid-swing pose for that leg in the middle (`sin`-shaped, up to `anim.stopLift`, 0.7), so the foot lifts and sets down instead of sliding.
- **The rest of the body** (spine, neck, head, tail) fades into the idle over `anim.stopBody` (0.3 s), and the settle spring still rocks it forward.
- **Moving again mid-stop**, the legs fade back to the gaits over `anim.fade`.
- The old "stride on to the lowest pose score" logic is gone from the tiger (`anim.stopMax`, the stop score table). The driver still has it, called with a zero window.
- **Checked in headless Chrome:** from a gallop, the planted hind foot stays put, the just-lifted front legs step down first, the last hind leg lands and steps in, and the tiger stands in about 0.4 s, then breathes. Stopping and restarting six times in a row, and a stop with `anim.stopSteps` off, end standing with no errors. `pnpm check` passes; the replay fixtures are untouched (view-only).
