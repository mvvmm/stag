# 1.5 Early Atmosphere: Implementation Plan

> Status: **done (2026-09-28).** Parent: [implementation-plan.md](../implementation-plan.md) step 1.5.

## Goal

The grey-box stops looking like a debug view and starts looking like a dark place, without losing readability:

- **Cold moonlight, warm druid:** dim blue-grey moonlight from above casting soft shadows, and a warm glow on the druid that makes the player the brightest, warmest thing on screen.
- **Fog:** distance fog for depth, plus height fog that pools along the floor, so pillars rise out of it and the void past the walls sinks into darkness.
- **Post-processing:** HDR, ACES tonemapping, bloom, vignette, color grading (curves), anti-aliasing, film grain, chromatic aberration and SSAO, each toggleable.
- **Readability tools:** a greyscale (value) view, stand-in threats, and a single switch back to flat grey-box lighting, so we can judge the look against what the game needs to show.

When it's done, every scene (greybox, gym, stress) is lit by one shared atmosphere rig tuned from the pane, the player and obstacles read clearly in color and in greyscale, and the simulation doesn't change: every replay fixture passes untouched. `pnpm check` must pass.

Out of scope:

- per-room or per-biome moods as data (6.2 / 9.2, once we know what varies)
- a LUT-based grade (considered at the art pass, 9.x, once real textures exist)
- player-light shadows, volumetric fog and god rays
- quality presets and graphics settings (9.6 / 16.1), and a frame-time budget: this step is "look first, measure later"
- real telegraphs and enemies (3.2 / 3.3): the stand-ins are view-only debug props
- the character model (1.6), which will get its own light and material tuning

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mood | **Cold moonlight + warm player** | Blue-grey key light and dark blue-black fog; a warm light on the druid. The cold/warm contrast keeps the player (and later telegraphs) easy to pick out. V Rising / Diablo IV at night. |
| Shadows | **Moon only** | One directional shadow map, soft (PCF), its frustum fit to the room. The player light doesn't cast shadows (a cube map is 6 renders a frame). |
| Fog | **Distance + height fog** | Babylon's built-in exp2 fog for depth, plus a height term from a WGSL material plugin: denser near the floor, thinning upward. It covers the void past the walls on every side, including the near (south) side the camera bounds leave visible. |
| Post | **DefaultRenderingPipeline, all extras** | HDR, ACES tonemapping, exposure/contrast, bloom, vignette, curves grading, MSAA (FXAA as an option), film grain, chromatic aberration (starts off or very low), and SSAO (SSAO2 rendering pipeline). Each is a pane toggle. |
| Grading | **Color curves now, LUT later** | Babylon's `ColorCurves` (shadows/midtones/highlights hue, density, saturation, exposure) as tunables. A LUT is noted for 9.x. |
| Player light | **Steady glow, slight breathing** | A warm point light above the druid's head with tunable range and intensity, and a slow, subtle intensity pulse: a living light, not a flickering fire. |
| Materials | **PBR + faint grid plugin** | Grey-box meshes switch to `PBRMaterial` (rough, non-metal), what glTF pack assets use, so lighting tuned now carries over to 1.6 / 9.x. The unlit grid shader goes away: the floor is a lit PBR surface with subtle procedural stone-like variation and faint grid lines, added by a WGSL material plugin, so distances still read while tuning movement. |
| Readability | **Value view, stand-in threats, checklist, A/B switch** | See the defaults below. |
| Scope | **Shared rig, global tunables** | One atmosphere rig built by the shell, used by every scene, with its numbers in tunable groups. No per-scene look yet. |
| Performance | **Look first, measure later** | No frame-time bar in this step. Every expensive part (shadows, SSAO, MSAA, bloom) has its own toggle, and the notes record the GPU time we see, for 9.6. |

### Defaults I picked (object if you disagree)

- **View-only.** Lights, fog, materials and post are rendering. Nothing touches the world, the RNG or the recording, so no replay fixture changes. The breathing pulse and film grain run on frame time; any view randomness is fine there (the dmath/Rng rules are for the sim), but the pulse is a plain sine anyway.
- **The rig lives in `render/atmosphere.ts`**, built once by the shell next to the scene (the scene persists across loads). It replaces the hemispheric and directional lights in `createScene`. Scenes talk to it through the context:
  - `ctx.setShadowCasters(meshes)` / the rig's shadow generator: the room view registers obstacles and the player body as casters; the floor and obstacles receive.
  - `ctx.setLightTarget(() => Vec3)`: what the player light follows (the room view passes the player mesh's interpolated position). No target, no player light.
  - Shadow frustum bounds come from the existing `setCameraBounds` room rect (plus the floor margin); scenes without bounds (stress) get a fixed box around the origin.
  - Everything the rig adds to a scene (casters, targets) is reset on every load, like the rest of the context.
- **Moon direction:** from the north-west and fairly high (about 55° above the horizon), so shadows fall toward the lower right of the screen and toward the camera a little, giving pillars a visible shadow side. Its heading and elevation are tunables.
- **Ambient:** a very dim cool hemispheric fill (blue-grey sky, near-black ground) so shadowed sides never go pure black. Its intensity is a tunable; the value view is how we check it.
- **Height fog math**, per pixel in the plugin: `density × exp(−max(y − base, 0) / falloff)`, integrated along the camera ray in closed form (the standard analytic height fog), mixed with the fog color on top of Babylon's exp2 term. The fog color matches the clear color so the edge of the floor has no seam. A pure TS mirror of the formula (`render/fogMath.ts`) is unit-tested and documents the shader. The plugin goes on every PBR material the rig knows (a `registerMaterial` helper, used by the room view and mesh sync's placeholder boxes).
- **Floor:** the floor mesh stays the big ground (room + walls + 20 m margin). The plugin adds low-frequency value noise (±5–8% albedo, and roughness) and grid lines at maybe a third of today's contrast, with the 5 m lines a bit stronger and nothing past the walls. Grid strength is a tunable, so it can go to 0.
- **Obstacle materials:** the existing per-type colors become PBR albedo (roughness ~0.9, metallic 0), slightly darkened so the moon doesn't wash them out. The player body stays orange-ish and gets a small emissive term so its silhouette survives the value view and bloom picks up its rim.
- **Occlusion fading keeps working:** it sets mesh visibility, which on PBR means alpha blending while faded; faded obstacles keep casting shadows. We check it doesn't sort badly against the fog.
- **Debug draw and the click marker are not graded.** Debug-draw meshes and the click marker opt out of fog and lighting, and debug draw renders after post (moved to a utility layer, or a rendering group the pipeline excludes, whichever works on WebGPU), so lines keep their true colors, don't bloom and don't get grain. The input overlay is HTML and isn't affected.
- **The debug free camera gets the same pipeline** (the pipeline is attached to both cameras), so looking around shows the real image.
- **Tunables** (new groups, all live in the pane; exact ranges set while building):
  - `light`: `moonColor`, `moonIntensity`, `moonHeading`, `moonElevation`, `ambientColor`, `ambientIntensity`, `playerColor`, `playerIntensity`, `playerRange`, `playerHeight`, `playerPulse` (amount), `playerPulseSpeed`
  - `shadow`: `enabled`, `mapSize` (1024/2048/4096), `softness` (PCF quality/filter), `bias`, `normalBias`, `darkness`
  - `fog`: `enabled`, `color`, `density` (exp2), `heightDensity`, `heightBase`, `heightFalloff`
  - `post`: `enabled`, `exposure`, `contrast`, `bloom`, `bloomThreshold`, `bloomWeight`, `bloomKernel`, `vignette`, `vignetteWeight`, `vignetteColor`, `grading` (on/off) plus curve values for shadows/midtones/highlights (hue, density, saturation, exposure), `msaa` (1/4), `fxaa`, `grain`, `grainIntensity`, `chromatic`, `chromaticAmount`, `ssao`, `ssaoRadius`, `ssaoStrength`
  - `floor`: `gridStrength`, `noiseStrength`
  `defineTunables` has no colors yet (numbers, booleans, selects), so this step adds a color spec: a hex string value, Tweakpane's color input in the pane, validated on set and in replays, and `Copy changes` printing the hex.
- **Readability tools** (debug only, in the pane's View folder, persisted like the other view settings):
  - **Atmosphere off:** one switch that turns the rig off and restores flat grey-box lighting (hemispheric + directional, no fog, shadows or post, the grid at full strength), for A/B comparison and for debugging collision and nav.
  - **Value view:** a greyscale post-process at the end of the chain (luminance of the final image). If the player, obstacles and floor still separate in grey, the scene reads.
  - **Stand-in threats:** a toggle that drops view-only props into the current room: a red-orange ground circle and cone (flat emissive decals, the rough danger language of 3.2) and a dark, dummy enemy silhouette (a capsule) near the player's start. They're meshes from the view, never entities, so nothing in the sim sees them.
- **Tests** (pure logic only): the height-fog formula (`fogMath.ts`: no fog above the base when the falloff is tiny, fog grows with ray length, matches a numeric integration), the breathing pulse (bounded, periodic), and fitting the shadow frustum to a room rect (covers every corner of the room plus margin for any moon direction). No Babylon in tests.

## Architecture

```
render/
  atmosphere.ts      the rig: moon + shadow generator, ambient, player light, fog, pipeline + SSAO,
                     value view, atmosphere-off fallback; tunables (light, shadow, fog, post, floor)
  fogMath.ts         pure: analytic height fog, pulse, shadow frustum fit        (tested)
  heightFog.ts       WGSL MaterialPluginBase: height fog on PBR materials
  floorMaterial.ts   PBR floor + WGSL plugin (noise, faint grid); replaces gridMaterial.ts
  scene.ts           createScene without its lights (the rig owns lighting)
  meshSync.ts        placeholder boxes use a PBR material registered with the rig
scenes/
  scene.ts           + ctx.setShadowCasters(meshes), ctx.setLightTarget(fn)
  arena.view.ts      PBR obstacle/player materials, registers casters/receivers and the light target
debug/
  pane.ts            View folder: atmosphere off, value view, stand-in threats
  standIns.ts        the stand-in threat props (view-only)
  debugDrawRender.ts rendered after post, no fog/lighting
core/tuning.ts       + color tunables (hex strings)
shell.ts             builds the rig once, resets its per-scene parts on load, attaches it to both cameras
```

Rule 1 holds: nothing in the sim changes, and `fogMath.ts` imports nothing from Babylon so its tests run in Node.

## Steps

1. `render/fogMath.ts` with tests (height fog, pulse, frustum fit). Color tunables in `core/tuning.ts` (with tests).
2. The rig's lights: moon, ambient fill, player light with the pulse, `ctx.setLightTarget`. Remove the lights from `createScene`.
3. PBR materials for obstacles, the player and mesh sync's placeholders. The floor material with its plugin; delete `gridMaterial.ts`.
4. Moon shadows: generator, casters/receivers through `ctx.setShadowCasters`, frustum fit to the room.
5. Fog: exp2 plus the height fog plugin on every registered material; clear color matches the fog.
6. Post: DefaultRenderingPipeline (HDR, ACES, exposure/contrast, bloom, vignette, curves, MSAA/FXAA, grain, chromatic aberration) and SSAO, on both cameras. Debug draw and the click marker kept out of fog and post.
7. Readability tools: atmosphere off, value view, stand-in threats.
8. Browser check (acceptance below) in the greybox, the gym and stress, both presets, with the free camera, a replay playback and a scene switch. Read the GPU time with each effect on/off and write it down. `pnpm check` and `pnpm build`.
9. Play it by hand and tune the defaults (mostly the light and fog balance, grading and the grid strength). Paste the pane's "Copy changes" back into the code.
10. Docs:
    - AGENTS.md: layout (`render/atmosphere.ts`, `fogMath.ts`, `heightFog.ts`, `floorMaterial.ts`, `debug/standIns.ts`), `setShadowCasters`/`setLightTarget` in rule 10, the atmosphere tools in the Dev tools section
    - implementation notes here, the LUT note for 9.x, tick 1.5, devlog entry `devlog/1.5-early-atmosphere/` (more screenshots than usual, since it's an atmosphere step: the greybox before/after with the A/B switch, the gym, the player in shadow and in the open, the value view, the stand-in threats, the fog swallowing the room's edge, a close free-camera view of height fog around the pillars)
    - commit "Early atmosphere (1.5)", push `1.5-early-atmosphere`, open a PR, get the checks and Preview green, and report the Preview URL (the user merges)

## Implementation notes

- **Verified in headless Chrome** (agent-browser, real WebGPU) on the dev server and the production preview (`?debug`):
  - The greybox, the gym and stress render with no WebGPU errors. Moon shadows fall to the lower right, as a moon from the north-west should; the player light follows the interpolated player and lights nearby pillars warm.
  - Resource counts (meshes, materials, lights, textures, overlay meshes, shadow casters) are identical after five restarts and a round trip through the gym, so the rig leaks nothing across loads.
  - The post chain is in order on the game camera and on the free camera, and stays in order through toggling SSAO, bloom and the free camera: SSAO's passes, then bloom, image processing, grain, chromatic aberration.
  - "Atmosphere off", the value view and the stand-in threats all switch live and persist in `stag.debug`. The click marker and debug-draw lines render in the overlay with their true colors, on top of the post-processed image.
  - Occlusion fading still works with PBR: the tall free-standing wall ghosts out (alpha blending) while the player is behind it.
- **Every replay fixture passed without `pnpm replay:update`**, so the simulation didn't change. The bundle grew by 4 kB gzipped.
- **Tuned in the browser against the plan's first guesses** (these are the new defaults): ACES tonemapping halves the brightness of a scene lit for no tonemapping, so `post.exposure` is 2.2. The moon went from 1.6 to 2.2, ambient from 0.35 to 0.55, the player light to 35 with a 10 m range at 2.5 m height, and the height fog to density 0.4, falloff 0.6, with a lighter fog color (`#141c2c`), so it reads as a haze on the floor and in the shadows. The rest of the hand tuning (step 9) is yours in play.
- **Bugs caught in the browser:**
  - `from` is a reserved word in WGSL, so the first height-fog shader failed to compile.
  - The camera/SSAO sync passed Babylon's live `pipeline.cameras` array into detach, which emptied it, so the default pipeline was silently detached from the first frame, and image processing fell back to the materials. The rig now detaches everything and reattaches in order (SSAO first) whenever the cameras or SSAO change. Babylon leaves a null slot in the camera's post-process list on every detach; they're skipped and only grow on manual toggles.
  - Wide banding rings around the player light came from SSAO: its copy of the scene color is 8-bit by default, which bands in the dark linear range. It's half float now. Dithering in image processing is on too.
- **Playtest fix:** on the Preview the top of the player's capsule was pure white. The light hangs about 1.3 m above it, so the head got several times the floor's light and bloomed. `setLightTarget` takes `{ exclude }` now, and the room view excludes the player's body (with its nose), which keeps its color and silhouette from the moon, fill and its own glow. Firefox on the user's machine runs the full stack at 120 fps.
- **Second playtest fix:** without the blown-out head the pool of light on the floor felt dull, and the breathing was invisible. The floor was the least reflective surface (albedo `#383a3a`, roughness 0.92) next to brighter obstacles. A lighter floor or a glowing wisp at the light didn't work: the first brightened the whole room under the moon, and the wisp floated detached above the head. What worked was a new `floor.roughness` tunable at 0.7 (a soft sheen where light hits), `light.playerIntensity` 70, and `light.playerPulse` ±12%.
- **Deviations from the plan:**
  - The floor plugin varies the albedo only, not the roughness.
  - Grading got its own tunable group, `grade`, next to `post`, and the floor's look is the `floor` group (grid, noise, the darkening past the walls, its color).
  - Debug draw's lines moved to the overlay scene (a `UtilityLayerRenderer`, which Babylon draws after post-processing). The depth-tested `grid` category stays in the game scene.
  - Stress registers no shadow casters and no light target, so it gets lights, fog and post but no shadows or player light.
- **Performance** wasn't measured reliably: headless Chrome was capped at 30 fps with or without the atmosphere, and the stats' GPU time swung between 1 and 19 ms with the full stack for the same settings. Without the atmosphere it's under 1 ms. SSAO and bloom looked like the biggest costs, and PCSS shadows cost clearly more than PCF. Real numbers wait for 9.6 on real hardware.
- **Readability findings:** the player is the brightest, warmest thing everywhere we looked, including tucked against a pillar in its shadow (its own light fills the moon shadow). The stand-in telegraphs read at a glance in color and in the value view. The dark stand-in enemy separates from the floor only by its eyes in the value view, so enemies will need a rim or lighter values (3.3 / 9.3).
- **Found, not fixed (pre-existing on `main`):** the occlusion fader doesn't fade the perimeter wall when it hides the player's lower half near the south wall.
- **For 9.x:** consider a LUT grade once real textures exist, player-light shadows, and quality presets.
