# 2.2 Ability Framework + Cat Auto Attack: Implementation Plan

> Status: **built (2026-10-02).** Parent: [implementation-plan.md](../implementation-plan.md) step 2.2.

## Goal

The first thing that deals damage, built on the machinery every later ability uses. 2.2 builds the **ability framework** in full: slots, cooldowns, the instant/windup/channel cast states, aiming, and abilities as data. One ability proves it in play: the Cat's **auto attack (AA)**, a single-target melee attack on the Training yard dummies. The framework doesn't know about the player, so enemies cast through it in 3.1.

Naming: it's the **auto attack** (AA), never a "swipe" (a later ability may be called that). An AA is always single target.

When it's done:

- in **WASD**, left-clicking a dummy makes the cat auto-attack it whenever the AA is ready and the dummy is in range. Holding left click while moving attacks whatever enemy the cursor is over, without stopping the movement for longer than the strike.
- in **MOBA**, right-clicking a dummy makes the cat chase it into range and keep attacking it, League-style. Holding right click acts like spamming it under the cursor.
- the AA plays the tiger's attack clip, enemies under the cursor are highlighted with an attack cursor, the current target has a ring under it, and damage numbers pop on the dummies
- the framework covers instant, windup and channelled casts, target/direction/point aiming, and effects as data. Unit tests cover all of it, including casts by a non-player caster.

Out of scope:

- **any ability besides the AA.** Slots `ability1`–`3` and `ultimate` exist but are empty, so their keys do nothing. Pounce comes in 2.4, the first damage ability in 2.5.
- **final keybinds** (2.3). Only the minimum moves: WASD's AA takes left click, so `interact` moves to `F` for now.
- **HUD** and cooldown display (2.6).
- **juice:** hit flash, hitstop, shake, knockback and better damage numbers (2.7).
- **enemy casting, telegraphs and AI** (3.1, 3.2). The framework is entity-agnostic and tested with a non-player caster, but no enemy casts in game yet.
- **death** and **player health** (3.x). Dummies still refill.
- **augments/modifiers** (later), and the loadout screen. The Cat's slots are set in code. Stats are read through one function, so modifiers can hook in later.

## Decisions

| Area | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| What the basic attack is | **An auto attack on `primary`**, defined and cast through the ability framework | Outside the 3 basics + ultimate of the loadout. Always single target. |
| WASD key | **Left click** (was right) | `interact` moves from left click to `F` until 2.3. |
| Targeting (both schemes) | **The enemy you click**, picked on screen | "It's whoever we left click, same as the moba control scheme's right click." No cursor-direction or nearest-enemy picking. |
| WASD target rules | **Sticky target, cleared by movement or another AA command** | Clicking a dummy keeps attacking it (held or released, even with the cursor drifting off) until a WASD movement or a click elsewhere. While left click is held, the enemy under the cursor becomes the target, so holding A + left click moves left, attacks whatever you hover whenever the AA is ready, and keeps moving. Abilities don't clear the target. |
| WASD out of range | **Nothing until in range** | No auto-walk in WASD. The target stays, and the AA fires the moment you walk into range. |
| MOBA target rules | **League** | Right-click an enemy: chase into range, attack, and keep attacking after release until the next order. Right-click the ground: move order. Holding right click = spamming it under the cursor. `S` clears the order. Abilities don't clear it. |
| Hover pick | **Screen space, recorded in the input frame** | The view picks the enemy under the cursor (anywhere on the body, like League). The input frame carries it as `hover`, so it's recorded and replays stay deterministic. The sim never raycasts. |
| Movement during the AA | **Rooted during the windup only** | Moving during the windup is ignored, not a cancel. Free again right after the strike. |
| Windup and range | **Always lands** (League melee) | Once the windup starts, the hit lands on its target whatever it does. |
| Framework scope | **Full framework, only the AA in game** | Slots, cooldowns, all three cast states, three aim kinds, effects as data. Instant and channel are covered by test-only definitions. |
| AA tempo | **0.7 attacks/s** | Base numbers start low and slow, to leave room for upgrades. |
| Visuals | **Attack clip, hover highlight + attack cursor, target ring, debug range ring** | Juice waits for 2.7. |

### Defaults I picked (object if you disagree)

**Cat AA numbers** (tunables group `catAA`):

- `attackSpeed` 0.7 /s, so the period is ≈1.43 s
- `windupShare` 0.18, i.e. a windup of ≈0.26 s. It's a share of the period, so attack-speed upgrades shorten the windup too, like League.
- `range` 1.2 m, edge to edge: from the cat's movement circle to the target's hurtbox
- `damage` 20 (the dummies have 1000 HP and refill, so they take 50 hits)

**Cooldowns and the input buffer:**

- An ability's cooldown starts when its cast starts, like League's attack timer.
- A pressed ability that can't start yet (on cooldown, or another cast running) is buffered for `abilities.buffer` 0.2 s.
- The AA doesn't need the buffer: it's driven by the target, not by presses.

**Stable entity ids (`uid`):**

- miniplex's `world.id()` is lazy (2.1's notes), so a new `uid` component holds a number from a per-world counter. The counter lives on a singleton `ids: { next }` entity, so snapshots restore it.
- Every spawned entity gets one through a `spawn(world, entity)` helper.
- The AA target, the MOBA attack order and the input frame's `hover` all hold uids.

**`faction: "player" | "enemy"`:** the player is `player`, dummies are `enemy`. Hover and targeting only consider enemies with `health` and a `hurtbox`.

**Abilities as data (`data/abilities.ts`, Babylon-free):** `{ id, aim, cast, cooldown, roots, effects }`

- `aim`:
  - `target` (an enemy uid, range-checked edge to edge)
  - `direction` (a unit vector toward the aim point)
  - `point` (a ground point clamped to range)
  - `self`
- `cast`:
  - `windup` seconds; 0 = instant
  - then an optional `channel { duration, interval }` that applies its effects every interval
  - whether movement cancels a channel is a flag
- `effects`:
  - `damage` on the target
  - `damage` in a hit shape (the 2.1 circle/cone/line) placed relative to the caster and the aim
- Numbers come from a `stats(caster)` function that reads the ability's tunables live, so 2.5's abilities and later modifiers change one place.

**Components:** a generic `caster` component, so enemies can have one too:

- `slots`: primary, ability1–3, ultimate → ability id + cooldown left
- `cast`: the running cast (slot, ability, phase, elapsed, resolved aim) or null
- `target`: the sticky AA target uid or null
- `queued`: the buffered press
- `casts`: a counter so the view can start one animation per cast, like `player.orders`
- the MOBA attack order lives with the move order on `player` (`order` becomes move | attack)

**Systems** (sim, deterministic; tick order):

1. **`playerControl`:** input becomes intent. It applies the WASD/MOBA target rules above, chases in MOBA, and queues ability presses. While a rooting cast runs, the desired velocity is 0.
2. **`casting`:** generic, for any caster. It ticks cooldowns, starts the AA when the target is in range and the slot is ready (or a queued ability when it can start), and advances the windup → channel phases. It applies effects through `applyDamage` and the 2.1 hit shapes.
3. `locomotion`, `patrol`, `dummy`, as before

While a cast runs, the caster's facing turns toward its aim at the movement turn rate, instead of following movement.

**MOBA chase:**

- The cat paths to the target with the existing `findPath`, and re-paths when the target has moved more than 0.25 m from the path's goal.
- It stops once in range and attacks, then chases again if the patrolling dummy walks off.
- Paths ignore solid bodies as before, so you slide round.

**Hover pick (view):**

- Each enemy gets an invisible pick cylinder: its hurtbox radius + 0.15 m, model height. That's cheaper and more forgiving than picking the model's meshes.
- The shell writes the picked uid with `input.setHover(uid | null)` each frame, like `setAim`.
- Replay format: a new optional `HOVER` field bit, so old fixtures stay valid (no hover).

**The AA's look:**

- **Attack clip:** the tiger's `attack` clip is time-scaled so its strike frame lands at the end of the windup. It fades in fast (~0.08 s) over the gait, then fades back out after the strike, sooner if the cat moves. It's driven by `caster.casts` and `cast.elapsed` on `viewTime()`.
- **Hover highlight:** a subtle outline on the hovered enemy. A `hoveredEnemy` signal swaps the cursor for an attack cursor (CSS cursor in WASD, the software cursor in MOBA).
- **Target ring:** a thin red ring under the current target in the overlay scene, hidden when there's none.
- **Debug draw:** a new `abilities` category (starts hidden) shows:
  - the AA range ring round the cat
  - a line to the target, green when it's in range
  - the cast phase and cooldown as text
  - effect shapes as they land
- **Cheat:** `noCooldowns`, a sim command (`systems/cheats.ts`) for testing.

## Architecture

```
data/abilities.ts      ability definitions (Babylon-free); the Cat's slots: primary = catAA
combat/abilities.ts    pure helpers: range check (edge to edge), aim resolution, effect application
systems/casting.ts     generic caster system: cooldowns, buffer, cast phases, effects
systems/playerControl  + WASD/MOBA target rules, MOBA chase, ability presses → caster.queued
ecs/world.ts           + uid, faction, caster; Player.order: move | attack
input/                 InputFrame.hover (uid | null), setHover; mmo: primary = Mouse0, interact = KeyF
replay/format.ts       + HOVER field bit (optional, old files stay valid)
render/                pick cylinders + hover outline (dummies.ts), target ring, attack clip (tiger.ts)
ui/                    attack cursor from a hoveredEnemy signal
```

## Implementation notes

- **The hover outline froze the game.** Babylon's outline renderer (`mesh.renderOutline`) builds an invalid WebGPU pipeline inside our SSAO prepass. Every frame with an outlined mesh failed validation, so hovering an enemy froze the picture until the cursor moved off (you hit it in play). The hover is now a **glow**: a WGSL material plugin (`render/highlightMaterial.ts`, like the rim light) adds a warm fill and a stronger fresnel edge to the final color, on lit and unlit materials alike. Each dummy gets its own copies of the Guardian's materials (`instantiateModel(id, { cloneMaterials: true })`) and its own stand-in material, so only the hovered one lights up.
- **The cursor** is set by the shell from `input.hover`, not from a `hoveredEnemy` signal: it's imperative like the software cursor (`ui/softwareCursor.ts`). Over an enemy it's three claw marks centered on the pointer, as the canvas's CSS cursor in WASD and as the drawn cursor while the pointer is locked in MOBA.
- **The MOBA attack order is `caster.target` + `player.chase`**, not a move | attack union on `player.order`. Both schemes share the sticky target the casting system reads. `chase` (set when the target came from a right-click) is what makes the cat walk: it paths to the target, repaths once the target has moved 0.25 m from the path's end, and drops the path once it's in range. WASD never sets `chase`.
- **`uid`s go on bodies only** (the player and the dummies, through `addWithUid`), not on obstacles or the room: only bodies are referred to by other state. The counter is a singleton `ids` entity. It shifts every later entity's index in `world.entities`, so all the fixtures' checksums changed. I checked that every fixture's transforms and health were identical before and after, every 30 ticks, then ran `pnpm replay:update`.
- **Tick order:** `playerControl` → `patrol` → `dummy` → `casting` → `locomotion`. Casting runs after every controller, so it can zero a rooted caster's desired velocity, and before locomotion, which turns the facing toward `cast.face` instead of the movement.
- **A channel that moving cancels** is cancelled by `casting` itself: it checks whether the controller asked to move this tick, so it works the same for AI casters.
- **The attack clip:** the model's `attack` clip is 3.96 s long (a paw slam, then a long roar). Sampling the paws offline showed both front paws raised at 0 s, slammed down by ~0.3 s and settled by ~0.65 s. The windup plays the clip 0 → 0.3 s (the slam lands with the hit), fading in over 0.07 s, then plays on 0.3 s more while fading out (`attackAnim` tunables). The tiger counts ticks itself to place the cast on the view's clock, which matches `viewTime` because seeks reload and fast-forward through real ticks.
- **The target ring** is in the overlay scene, so it draws over the dummy's lower body instead of disappearing behind it, like the click marker.
- **The `yard-auto-attack` fixture** was generated headless from a scripted input sequence rather than recorded in the browser: key and button timing through headless Chrome was unreliable in 2.1. It covers WASD walking up while holding the click on the static dummy, the sticky target after release, switching by hovering the patrol, a ground click dropping the target, a MOBA chase of the patrol (6 hits), and a ground right-click ending it.
- **Cheat:** "no cooldowns" in the Gameplay folder (`cheats.noCooldowns`, recorded like noclip).
- **The Training yard is the default scene** (asked for after the build: "make the default scene the one with enemies"), and it's first in the scene switcher. The arena and the gym stay in the switcher.
- `cf` was already on the latest beta (1.0.0-beta.12).

## Follow-up: playtest round (2026-10-02)

Three requests after playing:

- **"Enemy should not have red circle under them when AA'd."** The target ring is gone.
- **"In League there is kind of a cast time of an AA, and if you input a movement before that small cast time is complete, the auto will cancel."**
  - The windup is that cast time. It's already a share of the attack period (`catAA.windupShare`), so it scales with attack speed.
  - New on the ability data: `interruptible`. `casting` calls off an interruptible auto attack still winding up once its controller has **dropped or switched `caster.target`** (a ground right-click, `A`, a right-click on another enemy, an attack move elsewhere), and refunds the cooldown, so the next attack can start at once.
  - Re-clicking the same target doesn't cancel. The rule lives in the generic casting system, so enemy AI will get it for free.
- **Asked while settling the cancel rule: "Let's remove WASD entirely. Right click is the correct movement scheme for us."**
  - Players always start in the right-click scheme (`DEFAULT_PRESET` is `moba`, and the stored preset is only honoured in debug builds).
  - `M` no longer switches, and the pause menu no longer offers a scheme.
  - The WASD code stays, switchable from the debug pane, but new features don't consider it. Its hold-to-attack-while-moving rule is untouched.
- **"The attack animation is quite poor … I can FEEL when the damage is being dealt [in League]."**
  - The model's clip (a two-paw slam, seen mostly from above) is replaced by a **procedural right-paw hook**, built to read from the top-down camera:
    - over the first 55% of the windup the paw is drawn up and out to the side, the forearm folded, the body sitting back, the shoulders winding toward it
    - then a fast strike, fastest at the hit: the leg reaches forward and sweeps across in front, the forearm snaps straight, the shoulders whip back, the body lunges 0.3 m and pitches in, the head dips
    - it eases back over 0.3 s
  - The paw meets the target on the tick the damage lands. A cancelled windup fades out from where it was.
  - Numbers are `swipe` tunables. The timing is `swipePhase` in `locomotionAnim.ts` (tested).
  - Tuned from side and top-down frame sequences stepped tick by tick. The forearm folds with a negative `curl`. A `lift` of 150 threw the paw over the shoulder; 100 with `out` 70 gives a hook that reads from above.
  - Fixed on the way: a big view-time jump (a forward seek, or stepping many ticks in one frame) snapped the tiger and dropped the attack. The swipe's timing already ignores attacks that haven't started or are long over, so the snap leaves it.

## Follow-up: making the hit felt (2026-10-02)

You recorded the auto attack in League (Nidalee's cougar) and in stag. I compared them frame by frame (ffmpeg, every 2 frames, cropped to the fight). "League has so much life, whereas ours is so dull and lifeless."

- **League, per attack (~0.3 s to the hit):**
  - the whole body rears and lunges, and swipes come from different sides
  - the paw meets the enemy on the hit frame, where a bright yellow-orange starburst with crossed claw slashes flashes for ~0.15 s
  - the number punches in at the impact
  - a claw-trail arc lingers, and the body twists into the follow-through
- **Ours:** a leg-only hook mostly hidden under the cat's head from above, nothing at the point of contact, the number 2.6 m up over the bar, the same paw every time, and no reaction from the dummy.

What we built (you asked for all of it, with **random paws, not alternating**):

- **The impact** (`render/hitFx.ts`, overlay scene, additive, a pool of 8): a starburst that pops and fades in 0.18 s, and three claw scratches that rip across in 0.05 s and fade by 0.3 s. They sit where the paw meets the body: the hurtbox edge facing the cat, at 0.95 m. The scratches run the way that paw swept, with a small tilt per hit.
- **Damage numbers at the impact**, punched in at 1.7× and settling, instead of over the health bar.
- **The dummy reacts:** a warm white flash for about 0.1 s, then a recoil of 0.2 m away from the cat that tips it back and settles in ~0.3 s. The flash went in with the hover glow in the highlight plugin, and the glow gives way to it. The first try (85% white for 0.15 s) washed out the burst, so it's 50% now.
- **Full-body swipe:** the front rears up (16° nose up, 0.12 m higher) while the paw cocks, then drops into the pounce. `swipe.rear` and `swipe.rise`.
- **Random paw:** `swipeSide(casts)` hashes the cast count, so it looks random (about half each side, with repeats), but every replay and seek shows the same paw. The sim RNG stays out of the view. The left paw mirrors the sweep and the shoulder twist.
- **The trail:** a glowing orange TrailMesh streams off the striking paw tip from the strike start through half the release, then fades over 0.15 s. It's bright enough for the bloom, and it freezes while paused.
  - The first trail was millimetres wide: its generator node was under the model's centimeter-scaled rig, and TrailMesh takes its width from the generator's world scale. It's an unparented node now, moved onto the tip every frame.
- Hitstop and screen shake stay in 2.7.

## Follow-up: a deliberate cross-body swipe (2026-10-02)

"The actual mechanics of the tiger still feel quite wimpy, like throwing a wimp punch. Can we make a more deliberate cross-body swipe?"

The swipe was a forward reach with a small sideways hook, so it read as a jab. Now it travels across the body:

- **Wind-up** (`cock` 0.6 of the windup): the paw goes far out to its own side (`out` 80°) and up (`lift` 90°). The shoulders (`twist` 22°) and, new, the **whole body** (`turn` 18°) wind toward it, on top of the rear-up.
- **The hit lands as the paw crosses the middle**, straight at the target.
- **Follow-through**, new: `swipePhase` has a `through` part. Over the first 40% of the 0.42 s follow-through, the swing carries on past the other side (`across` 75°), ease-out, so it leaves the hit at full speed. The body and shoulders turn with it, and the head follows the paw (`look` 0.4). Then it all eases back.
- The trail runs from the strike through the swing past the target, so it draws the whole arc.

Checked tick by tick from the game camera: wound up with the paw high on its own side (ticks 4–12), a big arc across (14–16), crossing the dummy on the hit (17), then carried on past it with the body turning (19–26).

## Follow-up: a looser backside (2026-10-03)

"Can we fix the attack animation so the cat's backside is not so stiff during the attack?"

The rear-up, the pounce and the swing's turn rotated the whole body node, so the hindquarters swung round as one block and the hind feet slid. In this rig the hind legs and tail hang off the lower spine, and the front legs off the neck. So now the motion goes through the spine:

- **The turn bends through the spine.** The lower spine (hips, hind legs, tail) swings back against the swing by `swipe.hips` (35%): the weight shift. The front turns the rest, spread over the mid back (45%), upper back (35%) and neck (20%). Nothing yaws the whole body any more.
- **The rear-up and pounce mostly bend the back.** Only `swipe.bodyPitch` (40%) tilts the whole body. The rest lifts or drops the chest and upper back, so the back curves while the hips stay with the ground.
- **The tail follows:** it whips against the swing (`swipe.tailWhip`, 0.8° per degree of the front's turn) and lifts while the swipe winds and strikes (`swipe.tailLift`, 18°).
- The spine is posed before the paw, since the paw hangs off it.

## Follow-up: Nidalee's whole-body motion (2026-10-03)

"I'd give our AA animation a 5/10. Watch the League video frame by frame and try to mimic the movement. Consider the entire body, not just the attacking paw."

I studied two of Nidalee's cougar auto attacks at 60 fps, cropped tight and magnified. Both follow the same choreography, with the damage landing ~0.18 s in:

1. **Coil** (~0.1 s before the hit): she compresses, low and leaning back away from the target. The torso twists toward the striking paw's side, the paw is cocked far out at shoulder height, and the head draws back, eyes on the target.
2. **The hit is an explosive full-body extension:** she stretches long, forward and *up* at the target, the paw reaching it. This is the biggest motion seen from above.
3. **Follow-through** (0.05–0.15 s): still stretched, the paw sweeps on across with its trail as the body twists through.
4. **Recovery** (~0.2–0.3 s): she settles back down into the stance.

Ours did the opposite: it reared up while winding and dropped nose down at the hit, with a short lunge. So:

- **Coil:**
  - the body sinks `crouch` 0.1 m, pulls back `windBack` 0.2 m and tips nose down `coil` 8°
  - the paw is cocked wide and lower: `out` 95°, `lift` 50°
  - the head draws back (`dip` −8°) and turns back against the front's wind (`eyes` 0.8), so it keeps looking at the target
- **Strike:** a shorter, faster strike, the last 30% of the windup (`cock` 0.7). The body explodes forward and up, stretched long: `lunge` 0.45 m, `rise` 0.16 m, `rear` 18° nose up. The paw crosses the target on the hit.
- **Follow-through:** stays stretched while the swing carries on past (`across` 80°, `through` 0.3); the head follows the paw only now (`look`). Then it settles back down over the rest of the 0.4 s.
- Kept from before: the spine bend, the hips counter-swing and the tail whip.

Checked tick by tick from behind the cat, the way the League clip is framed:
- coil with the front bent away and the eyes on the target (ticks 9–13)
- unwinding into a long stretch at the target on the hit (16–17)
- swinging through to the other side (18–25)
- settling (29–34)

## Follow-up: the hind legs push off and hop (2026-10-03)

"Our cat still just slides forward along the ground when it attacks. The hind legs need to lunge forward, or even upward slightly."

The lunge moved the whole body node, so all four feet slid with it. The hind legs (thigh → calf → hock → foot, off the lower spine) now have a small two-bone reach, `reachFoot` in `tiger.ts`:
- First it folds the knee (the thigh one way, the calf twice the other) until the hip-to-foot distance matches the target, a few secant steps.
